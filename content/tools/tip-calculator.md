## How the tip calculator works

Enter your bill, pick a tip percentage (or type a custom one), and set how many people are splitting. The calculator adds the tip to the bill, applies an optional round-up, and shows the total, the tip in dollars, and the exact amount each person owes. Everything updates as you type.

The math is simple, but doing it in your head after dinner (and a drink) is where mistakes happen:

**Tip = Bill × (Tip % ÷ 100)** and **Total = Bill + Tip**

## Worked examples

**A solo dinner.** Your bill is $85 and you want to leave 20%. The tip is 85 × 0.20 = **$17.00**, so the total is **$102.00**. Turn on round-up and it becomes **$102.00** already — no change needed this time.

**Splitting four ways.** A group bill is $164 and you all agree on 18%. The tip is 164 × 0.18 = $29.52, making the total $193.52. Divide by 4 and each person owes **$48.38**. Splitting the $164 first and adding tip after would have left the server about a dollar short once everyone rounded down — which is why this tool adds the tip before dividing.

**Rounding up for simplicity.** A $46.30 bill at 20% comes to $55.56. Flip on round-up and the total becomes an even **$56.00**, nudging your effective tip to about 21% and saving everyone from counting coins.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Tipping on the post-tax total by accident.** It is not wrong, just slightly more generous. Know which number you are using.
- **Splitting the bill and tip separately.** Round-down drift leaves the server short. Combine, then divide.
- **Under-tipping on a discount.** The coupon is between you and the restaurant; the server earned the full-price tip.
- **Forgetting large-party auto-gratuity.** Many restaurants add 18–20% automatically for groups of six or more. Check the receipt before adding a second tip.
